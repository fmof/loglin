In lesson 17, each outcome was a single label. Now let's label every word of a sentence as N (noun), V (verb), P (preposition), or D (determiner). The context is a sentence, and each outcome is a whole tagging of it. A conditional log-linear model over outcomes like these is called a conditional random field (CRF).

Features. A transition feature such as N V fires each time a V follows an N. An emission feature such as V→like fires each time like is tagged V. (A small dictionary limits each word's tags.)

The trellis. Each tagging is a path from BOS to EOS. An edge's weight is exp of the total weight of its features, a path's weight is the product of its edges' weights, and Z totals all the paths. The forward-backward algorithm computes Z and the expected counts without listing the paths.

Try it out. Click "Disaggregate," which groups the paths by how they tag time flies. Set V→flies to 1. Which groups gain? Check "Show edge probabilities." How do the groups give you the probability of the BOS N edge?

Counts and the gradient. Click "Zero weights" and choose fruit flies like a banana. As with the shapes in earlier lessons, colors compare the model with the training data. A state or edge turns red if its expected count is smaller than its observed count, blue if larger, and gray if they match. ("Training data" on the left lists the observed taggings, and hovering shows both counts.) Why are some edges on the observed tagging gray? Why is the held-out sentence all gray?

Red doesn't mean wrong! Only something that was observed can be red. It's usually right, and the model is wrong not to give it enough probability. So it's good if a red path is at least the best path (the dashed gold line that "Show best path" draws).

Each slider's hint is still (observed count) − (expected count), totaled over the sentences. An edge's expected count is N × p(edge | words). Hover over a slider's handle to see its counts, in total and for this sentence. Why does V→travels have a hint, but not V→flies?
